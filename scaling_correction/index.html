νmax and Δν for stellar models

scaling_correction corrects the νmax and Δν that the scaling relations give for a stellar model. The νmax correction makes a model's νmax agree with the νmax–Δν relation observed by Kepler, and the Δν correction replaces the scaling Δν by the Δν of the model's radial modes. Both are linear formulas in log g, Teff, [Fe/H] and mass, so they can be used in grid-based modeling without computing oscillation frequencies.

log g is computed from M and R with log g⊙ = 4.438. The scaling relations use νmax,⊙ = 3090 μHz, Δν⊙ = 135.1 μHz and Teff,⊙ = 5772 K. The ± is the statistical error of the formula; single models scatter about it by more (see the formulas below).

How the calculator works

The page stores no models and no data, only the fitted numbers (about 5 kB): for f and fΔν and for each group, the five coefficients a–e, their covariance, the scatter of the models about the formula, and the range of the grid. They come from a least-squares fit to 28,767 MESA + GYRE models, and the Python package and the Fortran module use the same numbers. Everything is computed in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

The ± is the statistical error of the formula, from the covariance of the coefficients. Single models scatter about the formula by more, 0.9% (MS+SG) and 0.7% (RGB) for f and 0.7% and 0.3% for fΔν. A warning appears when an input lies outside the range of the grid.

Install

pip install git+https://github.com/Surendra-Bhattarai/scaling_correction

The only dependency is NumPy. The source is at github.com/Surendra-Bhattarai/scaling_correction.

Use in Python

import scaling_correction as sc

# corrections from log g (cgs), Teff (K), [Fe/H] and M (Msun)
f = sc.f_numax(logg=2.5, teff=4800, feh=0.0, mass=1.2)
fd = sc.f_dnu(logg=2.5, teff=4800, feh=0.0, mass=1.2)

# corrected values (muHz) from M and R in solar units
numax = sc.numax(mass=1.2, radius=9.0, teff=4700, feh=0.0)
dnu = sc.dnu(mass=1.2, radius=9.0, teff=4700, feh=0.0)

# statistical error of the fitted value
f, err = sc.f_numax(2.5, 4800, 0.0, 1.2, return_error=True)

Inputs can be NumPy arrays. Stars that are neither MS+SG nor RGB get NaN, and inputs outside the grid give a warning. f_dnu(..., normalization="raw") gives the convention of Sharma et al. (2016).

Use in MESA

Add mesa/scaling_correction.f90 to the src/ folder of your work directory and to its makefile, then in run_star_extras.f90:

use scaling_correction
...
numax_corr = scorr_numax(s% star_mass, s% photosphere_r, &
                         s% Teff, feh_initial)
dnu_corr   = scorr_dnu(s% star_mass, s% photosphere_r, &
                       s% Teff, feh_initial)

Mass and radius are in solar units; the functions return −1 for stars in neither group. Check the star_data names for your MESA version.

The formulas

Each correction is a linear function of four parameters, fitted separately to main-sequence and subgiant models (MS+SG, log g ≥ 3.5) and to red-giant-branch models (RGB, log g < 3.5 and Teff < 5600 K):

x = a + b log g + c log Teff + d [Fe/H] + e log(M/M⊙)

The νmax correction is f = νmax,emp/νmax,sc, where νmax,emp = 10AΔνB is the νmax of observed stars with the model's Δν (Kepler dwarfs and subgiants, and APOKASC-3 red giants). The Δν correction is fΔν = Δνmodel/Δνsc, with Δνmodel from a Gaussian-weighted fit to the radial modes, divided by the same ratio for the grid's solar-calibrated model. Errors are 1σ, with the models grouped by evolutionary track, and the rms is the scatter of single models about the formula.

Range and limits

  • Models from MESA r24.08.1 and GYRE 7.1, with an Eddington T–τ atmosphere and αMLT = 2.0, for M = 0.8–2.5 M⊙ and [Fe/H] = −3.0 to +0.4, up to Teff = 6500 K.
  • Valid for MS+SG models with 4400 ≤ Teff ≤ 6500 K and M ≤ 2.2 M⊙, and for RGB models with 4100 ≤ Teff < 5600 K and log g ≥ 1.9.
  • Red-clump and other core-helium-burning stars are not covered.
  • The corrections are meant for stellar models, for example in grid-based modeling. In a direct inversion of the scaling relations, where the mass is not known in advance, the νmax correction pulls νmax toward the observed relation; check this before using it that way.
  • The model Δν is not corrected for the surface effect. The default normalization by the solar-calibrated model removes most of it for Sun-like stars.
