I'm Surendra, a PhD candidate in the Department of Astronomy at Yale University, working with Sarbani Basu and Earl Bellinger. Stars ring like bells: sound waves and gravity waves travel through their interiors, and the frequencies they ring at tell us what is inside. I use those frequencies to study the cores of dead stars and the ages of star clusters.

Before Yale, I completed an integrated BS-MS dual degree in Physics at IISER Kolkata, with my Master's thesis at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore.

Outside research, I'm learning the flute and guitar, and I'm an active supporter of people living with type 1 diabetes.

02 Research

Two kinds of stars, one technique.

Oscillation frequencies are some of the most precise measurements in astronomy. I combine them with stellar models (MESA and GYRE) and space photometry from TESS and Kepler.

Current · PhD

Polluted white dwarfs

Many white dwarfs carry heavy elements in their atmospheres, debris from rocky planets they are still accreting. With MESA and GYRE models, I study how that pollution reshapes the envelope of pulsating (ZZ Ceti) white dwarfs and shifts their g-mode periods, and whether it can bias the masses and temperatures we infer from asteroseismology.

  • ZZ Ceti
  • g-modes
  • MESA + GYRE
  • accretion

Current · PhD

Red giants in open clusters

Stars born together in an open cluster share one age and one chemical makeup, which makes them a controlled experiment. I measure the oscillations of cluster red giants in TESS (and Kepler/K2) light curves to build a homogeneous seismic catalog, pin down cluster ages and helium abundances, and use them for Galactic archaeology.

  • TESS
  • solar-like oscillations
  • cluster ages
  • Galactic archaeology
